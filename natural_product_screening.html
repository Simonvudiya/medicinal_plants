Natural product screening and collaborative research

Summary of the Strathclyde Institute for Drug Research screening programme to the end of 2004, with a report on collaborative projects carried out through KNAS by Prof. Jacob O. Midiwo.

Screening programme

The Strathclyde natural product drug discovery group has focused on broad preliminary screening across many assays rather than follow-up chemistry.

22assays run on most samples
6more assays in progress
3standardised, ready when resources allow
80samples per 96-well plate

How samples are processed

All samples are dissolved in DMSO at 50 mg/ml and added to 96-well plates. Dilutions from these "mother plates" give a suitable screening concentration. The assays are mostly cell-based, a mix of binding and functional assays covering several therapeutic targets.

  1. Preliminary screen at a single concentration per extract
  2. Retest original sample and several lower concentrations
  3. Full dose-response curves for confirmed hits
  4. Fractionation and bioassay-guided isolation of active compounds

Assays and therapeutic targets

Thirteen assay areas are described in the programme summary.

AssayPurpose and method
Tumour necrosis factor α (TNF)Inflammatory cytokine; blocking it is expected to modify rheumatoid arthritis (as with Remicade, Humira, Enbrel). Cell-based assay measures the ability of extracts to prevent TNF-induced death of sensitised L929 fibroblasts.
Nuclear factor KB (NFKB)Transcription factor activated by inflammatory signals. Reporter-gene assay detects interference with NFKB activation (with University of St Andrews).
Cell adhesionBlocking adhesion of neutrophils and macrophages to blood-vessel endothelium may help asthma, arthritis and possibly cancer spread. Proprietary assays developed; pilot-stage screening only.
Cell proliferation: psoriasisLooks for selective inhibitors of proliferation in skin keratinocytes (NCTC cell line).
Cell proliferation: cancerCytotoxicity screening on Jurkat lymphoma, TE671 neuroblastoma and A375 melanoma cells. Specificity checked against normal mouse (L929) and human (HS-27) fibroblasts.
Anti-bacterial replicationHigh-throughput assay on deadbox protein A, a bacteria-specific enzyme (with University of Dundee). Conventional cultures: Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, M. aurum. Biofilm-prevention assay under development.
Anti-fungalA dermatophyte and a Candida strain used industrially for primary screening (with University of Aberdeen).
Trypanothione reductaseCloned trypanosomal enzyme, absent in mammals, targeting sleeping sickness and Chagas disease (with University of Dundee). Pilot-stage only.
Protease inhibitorsFive enzymes: chymotrypsin (pancreatitis), tryptase (inflammation), proteinase K, subtilisin A (possible antibacterial target) and a novel fungal protease.
AnticholinesteraseAcetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase inhibitors for Alzheimer's disease, using standard biochemical assays.
Muscarinic M4 receptorsM4 activators in the CNS are expected to be powerful analgesics. Radioligand binding on human cloned M4 receptors.
Anti-oxidantFree radical scavenging measured by the DPPH method.
Anti-diabeticGlucose uptake in adipocytes and skeletal muscle cells; inhibition of α-glucosidase, PTP-1B phosphatases and GPP-IV (as written in source; the GLP-metabolising enzyme). Screening still underway.

Anti-bacterial: Kenyan extracts 54 extracts retained

Minimum Inhibitory Concentrations (MICs) were established. 54 extracts remain for investigation because their MICs are 62.5 µg/ml or less and they showed no major cytotoxic effect on L929 cells. Eight of the active extracts are from Kenya:

Anti-fungal Not promising

3,280 extracts were tested against Candida kefyr and Trichophyton rubrum. Extracts were diluted 1:100 (5 mg/ml) and incubated at 35 °C for 48 h (C. kefyr) or 30 °C for 5 days (T. rubrum). A hit was growth ≤35% of control, or more than 2 standard deviations below the mean.

Many extracts appeared to inhibit C. kefyr, far fewer T. rubrum. At this high concentration, colour interfering with OD readings and non-specific effects can mimic inhibition.

In secondary screening most hits did not reproduce. Of nine that did, only 3 were active at 1:1000 (vs C. kefyr) and one was active against both fungi, at 1:100 only. Follow-up with fractions is being considered.

Protease inhibitors: chymotrypsin

5,121 samples were tested at 100 µg/ml. 18 samples with ≥60% inhibition were re-examined for concentration dependence. 4 were active and will be studied further; none is from Kenya.

Muscarinic M4 receptors Unconfirmed

4,801 samples were tested at 100 µg/ml in a radioligand binding assay with ³H-N-methylscopolamine. 190 appeared active (≥60% inhibition of binding). These activities are yet to be confirmed.

Anti-oxidant (partial excerpt)

Two active samples are from Kenya: Psychotria riparia (SIDR 1791) and Macaranga schweinfurthii (SIDR 3255). For SIDR 1791 binding fell as concentration rose, with an IC50 of 3.7 µg/ml.

The source pages are excerpts. Results for several assays (for example TNF, NFKB, cancer and anti-S. aureus) are not included in the text supplied.

Analyticon AG

The project isolates compounds that are distributed to companies for bioassay tests. 390 compounds have been isolated from extracts and characterised. They are being offered to companies, with some success. The company's report is enclosed.

University of Strathclyde

Extracts have shown activity as TNFα inhibitors, NFKB activation inhibitors, psoriasis-spread inhibitors, and in anti-cancer, anti-bacterial, anti-fungal, protease inhibitor, anti-cholinesterase and anti-oxidant activities. These hits are background for further study of the plants. The detailed Strathclyde report is enclosed.

University of Mississippi

The collaboration began last year and continues for five years under the agreement. A preliminary report has been requested and an update will follow on receipt.
